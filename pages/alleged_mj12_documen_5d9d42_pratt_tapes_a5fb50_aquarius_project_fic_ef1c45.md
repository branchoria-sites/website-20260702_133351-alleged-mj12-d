## Introduction

The unpublished *[Aquarius]({{'aquarius/' | relative_url }}) Project* manuscript occupies an awkward but important place in the history of [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}). It does not prove that the later MJ-12 papers were forged, nor does it demonstrate that the underlying UFO claims were false. Instead, it documents a period in which speculative fiction, alleged insider briefings and UFO investigation overlapped so closely that later claims of independent documentary discovery became much harder to evaluate.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Fiction Problem illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Within the pre-history revealed by the Pratt tapes, the manuscript shows that many ideas later associated with MJ-12—including an elite control group, Project Aquarius, presidential briefings and government management of recovered UFOs—were already being shaped into a narrative before the anonymous MJ-12 documents appeared publicly. That continuity creates what researchers often call the"fiction problem": when nearly identical story elements appear first in an unpublished novel and later in purportedly leaked classified papers, the independence of the later documents becomes a legitimate evidential question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span>

## What the manuscript was said to fictionalise

The *Aquarius Project* was an unpublished novel developed principally by UFO researcher William L. Moore with journalist Robert Pratt during 1981–82. Contemporary correspondence and later document collections indicate that the project was originally conceived because Moore believed he possessed fragments of an extraordinary story but lacked documentary evidence strong enough for a non-fiction book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/17474.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[files.bluebookfiles.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bluebookfiles.org.</span></span></span>

According to surviving descriptions, the manuscript attempted to fictionalise themes including:

* a secret government programme known as Project Aquarius;
* an exclusive oversight committee connected with UFO [recoveries]({{'recoveries/' | relative_url }});<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* classified presidential briefings;
* recovered alien craft and bodies;
* insider military contacts providing partial information rather than complete proof.</div>

One reported conversation between Moore and Pratt is especially revealing because Moore explained that he wanted to"get as much of the story out with as little fiction as possible." That remark suggests the novel was intended neither as pure invention nor as a documented historical account. Instead, it occupied an intermediate space where rumours, confidential conversations and speculation were woven into a fictional narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

This matters because the manuscript predates the public appearance of the famous MJ-12 papers by roughly two years.

## Why fiction does not prove forgery

The existence of the manuscript should not be overstated. Writing a novel based on UFO rumours is not, by itself, evidence that later documents were fabricated.

Several alternative explanations remain logically possible:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the novel could have been based on genuine insider information that Moore believed but could not document;
* authentic classified material might have inspired both the novel and later leaked documents independently;
* fictional treatment may simply have reflected uncertainty rather than deception.</div>

For that reason, historians generally avoid treating *The Aquarius Project* as a"smoking gun". The manuscript cannot establish who created the later MJ-12 papers, when they were created or whether any genuine classified information existed behind the stories.

The evidential value of the manuscript lies elsewhere. It demonstrates that the narrative architecture associated with MJ-12 was already circulating inside a relatively small network of researchers and alleged government contacts before the famous documents emerged. That reduces the force of arguments that the later papers introduced completely novel information unavailable to anyone outside government.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Fiction Problem illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## How story continuity damages claims of independence

The principal historical significance of *The Aquarius Project* is the continuity it reveals.

If the later MJ-12 documents had introduced ideas wholly absent from earlier discussions, supporters could argue that the papers represented an unexpected archival discovery. Instead, researchers comparing the Pratt tapes, Moore's correspondence and later MJ-12 material have identified substantial overlap in both structure and content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span>

The recurring elements include:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Project Aquarius** as the framework for official UFO secrecy.
* **An elite management committee** overseeing the subject.
* **Presidential transition briefings**, particularly around the Truman-to-Eisenhower period.
* **Recovered flying saucers and biological remains.**
* **Restricted dissemination to a tiny circle of officials.**</div>

Seen individually, none of these similarities proves document fabrication. Taken together, however, they show that many supposedly startling features of the MJ-12 papers had already existed as working narrative components inside Moore's [research circle]({{'research-circle/' | relative_url }}).

This is precisely the concern raised by later critics: if authors, researchers and alleged insiders had already exchanged these concepts while discussing both fictional and non-fictional projects, then similarity between the eventual MJ-12 documents and earlier expectations loses much of its evidential value. Rather than confirming [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}), matching details may simply reflect a shared pool of circulating ideas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Fiction Problem illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_pratt_tapes_a5fb50_aquarius_project_fic_ef1c45-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The feedback loop between rumours and documents

The *Aquarius Project* illustrates a broader methodological problem in UFO history: information does not necessarily move in a single direction from government [archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) to researchers.

Instead, the evidence surrounding Moore, Pratt and Richard Doty suggests the possibility of repeated feedback:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Researchers develop hypotheses from historical investigation.
2. Alleged insiders reinforce or embellish those ideas.
3. The ideas are incorporated into fictional or semi-fictional writing.
4. Similar themes later appear in purported classified documents.
5. The documents are then cited as independent confirmation of the original ideas.</div>

## What this means for evaluating the alleged MJ-12 papers

For historians of the MJ-12 controversy, the fiction problem is not that someone wrote a UFO novel. It is that the same individuals who were simultaneously researching Roswell, communicating with purported intelligence insiders, discussing Project Aquarius and drafting fiction later became central figures in promoting allegedly authentic classified documents containing many of the same concepts.

That overlap does not resolve the authenticity debate on its own. Genuine intelligence operations have occasionally used fictional cover stories, and fiction has sometimes drawn upon authentic classified knowledge. Nevertheless, the existence of *The Aquarius Project* substantially weakens any claim that the MJ-12 narrative appeared suddenly and independently from an unknown archival source.

Instead, the surviving evidence points to a much more complicated pre-history in which rumours, research, alleged insider briefings and fictional storytelling evolved together.
