## Introduction

The Air Force Office of Special Investigations (OSI) conclusion that the alleged MJ-12 papers were fabricated resolved one question but left another unanswered. The documents could be rejected as inauthentic without identifying who created them, who distributed them, or why they entered circulation. That distinction has shaped decades of debate. While the FBI file records the Air Force's judgement that the papers were"completely bogus" and closes the classified-document inquiry, it does not identify a forger or set out an investigative narrative explaining the documents' origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Forger Gap illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This gap matters because [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) and authorship are different historical questions. A document may be shown to be false through forensic or institutional review, yet the identity and motives of its creator can remain unresolved. In the MJ-12 case, that unresolved [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}) has allowed competing explanations—including deliberate hoaxes, intelligence-related disinformation, and private fabrication—to persist long after the authenticity of the documents was challenged.

## Why authenticity and authorship are separate questions

The OSI finding addressed whether the papers represented genuine classified government records. According to the FBI's publicly released files, the answer was no: the document was considered fabricated and the investigation into possible disclosure of classified material was closed. That administrative decision did not require investigators to establish criminal responsibility or reconstruct the document's complete history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span>

From a historical perspective, those are separate tasks:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Authenticity** asks whether the document is what it claims to be.
* **Authorship** asks who created it.
* **Provenance** asks how it moved from its point of creation into public circulation.
* **Motive** asks why it was produced and distributed.</div>

Rejecting authenticity therefore does not automatically answer the remaining three questions. In many document-forgery cases, investigators establish that a record is not genuine without ever identifying its creator.

## How the lack of a named forger fuelled later speculation

Because no official account identified the creator, competing narratives emerged within UFO research.

One broad interpretation argues that the documents were simply the work of private hoaxers familiar with Cold War military terminology, archival practices and UFO literature. Under this view, the absence of a named forger reflects the practical reality that anonymous fabrications are often difficult to trace years after their creation.

Another interpretation proposes some form of official disinformation. This idea has often centred on former Air Force OSI Special Agent Richard Doty, whose documented involvement in misleading UFO researcher Paul Bennewitz made later allegations of government-sponsored deception more plausible to some researchers. However, despite extensive discussion in UFO literature, no official investigation has publicly concluded that Doty or any government agency authored the original MJ-12 papers. The evidence remains circumstantial and heavily disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

A third position holds that several different forged documents may have been created by different people over time rather than by a single author. Researchers have long noted that"MJ-12" eventually became a label attached to numerous purported classified papers of varying quality, making it difficult to speak of one unified forgery campaign.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dokumen.pub/case-mj-12-1628157453-9781628157451.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dokumen.pub">[dokumen.pub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dokumen.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case MJ-12 1628157453, 9781628157451Stan Friedman, one of the leading proponents of MJ-12, and I had clashed over the authenticity of the...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Forger Gap illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the evidence remains inconclusive

The principal obstacle is that no documented chain of custody exists for the original creation of the documents.

Instead, researchers have reconstructed events from:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* anonymous mailings;
* recollections by participants;
* interviews conducted years later;
* comparisons between different versions of the papers;
* archival discoveries; and
* testimony from individuals whose own credibility has often been questioned.</div>

This evidential landscape makes firm attribution difficult. Even researchers who strongly reject the authenticity of the papers have frequently disagreed about who produced them.

## What a fuller provenance investigation would require

To move beyond speculation, a convincing provenance investigation would need evidence extending well beyond the authenticity question addressed by OSI.

Such an investigation would ideally establish:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the earliest verifiable physical copy of each disputed document;
* the complete chain of possession before public release;
* typewriter, printing or photographic evidence linking documents to specific equipment;
* correspondence showing drafting, editing or distribution;
* independent witness testimony corroborating authorship; and
* documentary records connecting any alleged participants with the production process.</div>

Very little of this evidence has entered the public record. Instead, debates have largely relied on retrospective interviews, memoirs and competing interpretations of fragmentary archival material.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Forger Gap illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_unresolved_forger_qu_aa8155-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The enduring significance of the"forger gap"

The unresolved authorship question has had consequences far beyond the original documents.

Supporters of MJ-12 have sometimes argued that the absence of a proven forger leaves open the possibility that authentic material was wrongly dismissed. Most historians and document researchers reject that reasoning, noting that failure to identify a forger is not positive evidence of authenticity. A forged document can remain anonymous indefinitely.
