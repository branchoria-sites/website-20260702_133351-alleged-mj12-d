## Introduction

The first meeting of the US [National Security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) Council (NSC), held on 26 September 1947, is one of the real historical events that gives [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) an appearance of plausibility. James Forrestal, the first Secretary of Defense, and Rear Admiral Roscoe Hillenkoetter, the first Director of Central Intelligence, were both present in an institution specifically created to handle the nation's most sensitive security issues. That coincidence has encouraged later speculation that the NSC served as a cover or birthplace for a secret UFO control group.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_forrestal_26f0e9_first_nsc_meeting_1c00b2-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="First NSC illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_forrestal_26f0e9_first_nsc_meeting_1c00b2-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_forrestal_26f0e9_first_nsc_meeting_1c00b2-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The documentary record, however, points in a different direction. The first NSC meeting unquestionably demonstrated the emergence of a permanent post-war national security [bureaucracy]({{'bureaucracy/' | relative_url }}) operating under strict secrecy. Yet the surviving minutes, official records and subsequent NSC documentation show a council occupied with organising national security procedures, intelligence oversight and Cold War governance—not with extraterrestrial [recoveries]({{'recoveries/' | relative_url }}) or an alleged Majestic-12 organisation. The historical atmosphere resembles what the MJ-12 story imagines, but the evidence does not support the existence of such a body.

## Who attended on 26 September 1947

The inaugural NSC meeting took place at the White House just over a month after the National Security Act of 1947 came into force. Official minutes identify the participants with unusual precision, providing an important benchmark for evaluating later conspiracy claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian225</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minutes of the First Meeting of the National Security...Washington, September 26, 1947. * President Harry S. Truman, presiding.
* [James Forrestal]({{'forrestal/' | relative_url }}), Secretary of Defense.
* Robert A. Lovett, Acting Secretary of State.
* Kenneth C. Royall, Secretary of the Army.
* John L. Sullivan, Secretary of the Navy.
* W. Stuart Symington, Secretary of the Air Force.
* Arthur M. Hill, Chairman of the National Security Resources Board.
* Sidney W. Souers, Executive Secretary of the NSC.
* Rear Admiral Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter, Director of Central Intelligence, attending as an observer and adviser.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian225</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minutes of the First Meeting of the National Security...Washington, September 26, 1947. For readers familiar with the alleged MJ-12 documents, the overlap is striking. Forrestal and Hillenkoetter both appear prominently in MJ-12 narratives, making the meeting seem like a plausible setting for highly classified discussions. However, the official record also shows exactly why they were together: the National Security Act had just created a new framework for coordinating defence, diplomacy and intelligence at Cabinet level.

The presence of these individuals therefore demonstrates administrative reality rather than evidence of an undisclosed UFO committee.

## What"utmost secrecy" meant in context

One phrase from the first meeting has received disproportionate attention in later conspiracy literature. Among the Council's first procedural decisions was a simple instruction:

>"The affairs of the Council will be conducted with the utmost secrecy."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian225</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minutes of the First Meeting of the National Security...Washington, September 26, 1947. PARTICIPANTS. Members Present; The President of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 1947</span></span></span>

Taken in isolation, this language can sound dramatic. In its historical context, however, it reflected the NSC's institutional purpose rather than an indication of hidden extraordinary projects.

The Council had been created to coordinate military planning, intelligence assessments and foreign policy at the beginning of the Cold War. Its discussions routinely involved classified military capabilities, diplomatic negotiations, intelligence reporting and strategic planning. Protecting those discussions was therefore an expected administrative requirement.

The same meeting illustrates this ordinary national security function. The Council approved its operating procedures, established arrangements for its staff, approved an initial directive concerning the newly created Central Intelligence Agency, and received a global intelligence review from Director Hillenkoetter. These agenda items are entirely consistent with building a functioning national security system after the Second World War.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian225</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minutes of the First Meeting of the National Security...Washington, September 26, 1947. ## Where the real NSC records stop helping MJ-12

The first NSC meeting is valuable precisely because official documentation survives. The minutes identify participants, summarise agenda items and record formal actions. They provide an authentic picture of how senior officials actually conducted their business in September 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian225</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minutes of the First Meeting of the National Security...Washington, September 26, 1947. PARTICIPANTS. Members Present; The President of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 1947</span></span></span>

This transparency creates an important evidential limit for MJ-12 claims.

If the alleged Majestic-12 organisation had been formally established through the NSC or had reported directly to it, historians would expect at least indirect traces within the surrounding documentary ecosystem: agenda references, supporting memoranda, follow-up directives, funding discussions or cross-references in later files. Instead, the official records concern institutional development, intelligence coordination and policy-making.

Subsequent NSC meetings reinforce this pattern. Forrestal and Hillenkoetter continued to work together on sensitive issues—including covert operations, CIA organisation and Cold War strategy—but these discussions remain identifiable as national security business within the evolving responsibilities of the Council rather than evidence of a compartmented UFO authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d283" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian - History State GovThe following notes contain a brief summary of t...</span></span></span>

The existence of genuine secrecy therefore cuts both ways. It explains why later narratives found the NSC an attractive setting for fictional or disputed documents, yet the surviving archival record consistently documents real Cold War priorities instead.

## Why the first NSC meeting matters to the MJ-12 story

The inaugural NSC meeting helps explain why the alleged MJ-12 papers feel historically convincing to many readers.

Several authentic elements align neatly with the mythology:

* Forrestal genuinely occupied the centre of post-war defence reorganisation.
* Hillenkoetter genuinely headed the newly established CIA.
* The Council genuinely operated under stringent security rules.
