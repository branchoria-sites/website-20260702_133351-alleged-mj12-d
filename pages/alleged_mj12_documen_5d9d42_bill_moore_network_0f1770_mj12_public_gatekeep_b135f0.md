## How interpretation became influence

The trio's importance rested less on exclusive possession of documents than on their role in explaining what the documents supposedly meant.

The alleged Eisenhower Briefing Document contained names of genuine senior military officers, intelligence officials and scientists. Friedman, drawing on his technical background and extensive historical research, argued that many details were consistent with Cold War security practices and therefore deserved serious consideration. He devoted years to answering criticisms concerning typography, terminology and historical context, maintaining that some objections were weaker than critics claimed while also acknowledging that later MJ-12 papers included obvious forgeries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanton_T._Friedman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Stanton T. Friedman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stanton T. Friedman</span></span></span>

Moore's contribution was different. Having already become associated with [Roswell research]({{'feedback-loop/' | relative_url }}), he supplied historical context and promoted the idea that the documents fit an existing pattern of government secrecy surrounding recovered UFO material. His previous work meant that many readers interpreted MJ-12 as an extension of the Roswell narrative rather than as an isolated anonymous document release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

Shandera served as both recipient and presenter. Because the original film arrived anonymously, his account of its arrival became part of the evidential chain. In effect, confidence in the documents depended partly upon confidence in his description of how they entered the public record. Unlike an officially documented archival accession, there was no independently verifiable chain of custody linking the papers to a government source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span>

This combination meant the researchers did more than publish documents. They framed chronology, proposed connections between separate discoveries, assessed [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) and established which anomalies should be viewed as evidence of secrecy rather than evidence of fabrication.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_bill_moore_network_0f1770_mj12_public_gatekeep_b135f0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Gatekeepers illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_bill_moore_network_0f1770_mj12_public_gatekeep_b135f0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_bill_moore_network_0f1770_mj12_public_gatekeep_b135f0-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

## Why the gatekeeper question still matters

The lasting significance of Moore, Shandera and Friedman lies not simply in whether they were correct or mistaken, but in the mechanism by which the MJ-12 story entered public consciousness.

Because virtually every early public discussion flowed through this small network, later researchers inherited both the documents and the interpretative framework that accompanied them. The anonymous origin of the papers meant there was no independent institutional context capable of replacing the trio's narrative. Their explanations therefore became part of the evidence readers were expected to evaluate.

Subsequent controversies reinforced this dynamic. Moore's later acknowledgement that he had participated in aspects of Air Force-related disinformation activities involving other UFO investigations inevitably caused critics to revisit his role in the MJ-12 affair, even though no definitive evidence has emerged proving that he authored the original documents. Conversely, supporters have argued that the presence of intelligence operations within UFO research makes anonymous document releases more plausible rather than less so. Both interpretations demonstrate how difficult it became to separate documentary analysis from the reputations of the people who first introduced the material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_reports_and_disinformation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Das UFO-Phänomen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO reports and disinformation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO reports and disinformation</span></span></span>

The result is that Moore, Shandera and Friedman occupy a distinctive place in MJ-12 history. They were not merely investigators of disputed documents; they became the principal public filters through which those documents were understood, defended, questioned and debated.
