## Introduction

The FBI's handling of the alleged MJ-12 papers is often presented as one of the strongest official arguments against their [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}). However, it is important to understand exactly what the Bureau did—and did not—conclude. The FBI did not conduct a historical investigation into whether a secret Majestic-12 committee ever existed. Instead, it opened a file because documents marked as highly classified had appeared publicly, creating a potential security concern. After consulting with the U.S. Air Force's Office of Special Investigations (OSI), the Bureau closed the matter when the documents were reported to be fabricated and repeatedly marked the file as"bogus."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Majestic%2012" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI+2MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12Majestic 12 In 1988, two FBI offices received similar versions of a memo... document to be a fake...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="FBI File illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This distinction matters because the FBI file is evidence about the government's treatment of the documents themselves, not independent proof about every claim associated with UFOs or government secrecy. It shows that the documents entered official channels as suspected forgeries rather than as recognised [classified records]({{'secret-trails/' | relative_url }}).

## What the FBI was asked to check

The FBI's Majestic-12 file originated from a practical law-enforcement question, not an attempt to solve the Roswell mystery. In 1988, Air Force investigators became concerned after a document labelled"[Top Secret]({{'top-secret/' | relative_url }})" circulated publicly. If the document had been genuine, its disclosure could have represented an unauthorised release of classified information.

The Bureau therefore opened a file under an espionage-related classification to determine whether a federal security issue existed. The focus was straightforward:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Was the circulated document an authentic classified government record?
* Had genuine classified information been leaked?
* Was further investigation necessary?</div>

## How the"bogus" conclusion appears in the file

The FBI Vault now publishes the released Majestic-12 file. Numerous pages carry a prominent"BOGUS" stamp, making it one of the most frequently cited official references in debates over MJ-12. The accompanying case summary states that the investigation was closed after it was learned that the document was"completely bogus."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Majestic%2012" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI+2FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12Majestic 12 In 1988, two FBI offices received similar versions of a memo... document to be a fake...</span></span></span>

The chronology reflected in the released records is significant:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Air Force investigators referred the matter because of concerns over an apparently classified document.
* The FBI sought input from the Air Force.
* The Air Force Office of Special Investigations advised that the document had been fabricated.
* The FBI closed its inquiry because no genuine classified document was involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.muckrock.com/news/archives/2017/jun/05/fbi-majestic-12/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muckrock.com">[MuckRock]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muckrock.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">fbi majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic-12 documents, by “an individual at the school.” The FBI and... document “was fabricated” and “completely bogus.” Having been...</span></span></span></div>

The"bogus" annotation therefore reflects the official disposition of the questioned document within the case file. It was not merely an informal opinion written in the margin but part of the Bureau's documented handling of the investigation.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="FBI File illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

## What the FBI finding does not prove

The file is sometimes overstated by both supporters and critics.

The FBI did **not** establish that every document ever associated with MJ-12 originated from the same source or was forged by the same individual. The released records do not identify the creator of the alleged briefing papers.

Nor did the Bureau produce a detailed forensic report explaining every historical or typographical inconsistency in the documents. The file records an administrative conclusion that the questioned material was fabricated, not an exhaustive scholarly authentication study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Majestic%2012" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12Majestic 12 In 1988, two FBI offices received similar versions of a memo... document to be a fake...</span></span></span>

Likewise, the file does not prove that no classified UFO programmes have ever existed. Its scope was much narrower: determining whether the specific documents submitted to investigators represented genuine classified government records.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="FBI File illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_fbi_bogus_finding_85393a-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the FBI file remains important

Among official sources, the FBI file carries unusual weight because it records how a federal law-enforcement agency handled [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) when they were presented as potentially classified material.

For historians and document examiners, its importance lies less in the word"bogus" itself than in the bureaucratic process behind it. The documents were evaluated as questioned records, referred to the appropriate defence authorities, and closed as fabricated rather than authenticated government papers. That treatment directly contradicts claims that the documents entered official channels as recognised secret records.
