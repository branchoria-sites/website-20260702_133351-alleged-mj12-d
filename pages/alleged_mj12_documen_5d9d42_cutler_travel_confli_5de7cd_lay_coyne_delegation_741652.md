## Introduction

One of the strongest procedural objections to the alleged MJ-12 Cutler–[Twining]({{'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum is not simply that Robert Cutler was abroad on 14 July 1954. It is that he had already established a formal delegation arrangement for [National Security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) Council (NSC) business before leaving Washington. Contemporary records show that, on 3 July 1954, Cutler instructed his senior deputies, James S. Lay and J. Patrick Coyne, on how routine NSC administrative work was to be handled during his absence. That creates a practical expectation: if an ordinary scheduling memorandum genuinely originated from the NSC staff while Cutler was overseas, it would more naturally have been issued under the delegated authority of Lay or Coyne rather than bearing Cutler's name.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a memorandum from Robert Cutler to Gen. This delegation issue matters because the disputed memorandum presents itself as routine administrative correspondence rather than an exceptional presidential directive. The question is therefore not whether Cutler could ever communicate while travelling, but whether the document behaves in the way genuine NSC paperwork normally would under an established delegation arrangement.

## Cutler's 3 July absence instructions

The National [Archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) identifies an authentic memorandum dated 3 July 1954 in the NSC Staff Papers in which Cutler instructed James S. Lay and J. Patrick Coyne on handling NSC administrative matters during his trip to military installations in Europe and North Africa. The same archival review also identifies Cutler's genuine report to President Eisenhower after returning from that journey, confirming that he was away between 3 and 15 July 1954.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a memorandum from Robert Cutler to Gen. ## How delegated NSC paperwork should behave

The disputed Cutler–Twining memorandum is brief. It does not announce a new policy or record a presidential decision. Instead, it simply changes the timing of an already planned briefing and instructs General Nathan Twining to adjust his arrangements.

That kind of communication resembles ordinary staff coordination rather than a unique document requiring the personal involvement of the President's Special Assistant. In a bureaucratic system such as the Eisenhower White House, delegation existed precisely so that this sort of scheduling and coordination could continue while senior officials travelled.

James S. Lay was not a junior clerk. As Executive Secretary of the National Security Council, he routinely managed the Council's administrative processes and records. J. Patrick Coyne likewise held significant responsibilities within the NSC staff, particularly concerning internal security matters in the Eisenhower years. Both men were experienced officials capable of issuing or coordinating routine NSC correspondence within delegated authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp90-00708r000300040001-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE DCI HISTORICAL SERIES CIA SUPPORT FUNCTIONSNSC staff changes: James S. ## Can alternative explanations resolve the problem?

Supporters of the document have suggested various possibilities, including that the memorandum may have been drafted before Cutler departed, transmitted from abroad, or left unsigned because it was merely an internal routing document.

These explanations face two practical difficulties.

First, the memorandum is dated 14 July, near the end of Cutler's documented overseas trip rather than before it began. A pre-prepared draft would still require an explanation for why it carries a date when Cutler was absent and why it concerns a meeting scheduled only two days later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a memorandum from Robert Cutler to Gen. Nathan Twining, dated July</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsMemorandum for General Twining, from Robert Cutler, Special Assistant dat...</span></span></span>

Secondly, the delegation memorandum demonstrates that an established mechanism already existed for handling exactly this sort of continuing administrative business. If Lay and Coyne had authority to manage NSC affairs during Cutler's absence, the disputed memorandum's attribution to Cutler becomes less consistent with normal office procedure.

For these reasons, historians who question the [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) of the alleged MJ-12 papers generally regard the delegation issue as reinforcing—not replacing—the broader timing problem surrounding the document.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_cutler_travel_confli_5de7cd_lay_coyne_delegation_741652-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Delegation illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_cutler_travel_confli_5de7cd_lay_coyne_delegation_741652-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_cutler_travel_confli_5de7cd_lay_coyne_delegation_741652-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
