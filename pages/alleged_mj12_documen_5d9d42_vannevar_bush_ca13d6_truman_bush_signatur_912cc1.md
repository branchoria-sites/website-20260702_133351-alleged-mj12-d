## Introduction

One of the most frequently cited objections to [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) concerns neither UFO claims nor the alleged committee itself, but the provenance of a presidential signature. Critics argue that the signature attributed to President Harry S. Truman on one of the core MJ-12 papers was not independently written for that document at all. Instead, they contend that it was copied from an authentic letter Truman sent to [Vannevar Bush]({{'bush/' | relative_url }}) on 1 October 1947 and then incorporated into a forged document.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_vannevar_bush_ca13d6_truman_bush_signatur_912cc1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Signature Claim illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_vannevar_bush_ca13d6_truman_bush_signatur_912cc1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_vannevar_bush_ca13d6_truman_bush_signatur_912cc1-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This issue matters because it shifts the [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) debate from broad speculation to a specific, testable claim. If a genuine presidential signature from routine White House correspondence was recycled into an alleged classified directive, it would suggest that authentic archival material was used to manufacture the appearance of official [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}). The dispute therefore centres on source reuse: whether genuine Truman–Bush correspondence became raw material for creating documents that appeared historically convincing.

## The genuine Truman–Bush correspondence

The copied-[signature]({{'signature/' | relative_url }}) allegation depends on the existence of an authentic piece of presidential correspondence.

Among Truman's surviving papers is a genuine letter sent to Vannevar Bush on **1 October 1947**. Bush, then one of the United States' leading scientific administrators, corresponded regularly with the White House on science and [national security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) matters, making authentic Truman–Bush letters entirely unsurprising within the historical record. The disputed MJ-12 papers therefore did not require a forger to invent Bush's relationship with Truman; that relationship already existed and is well documented in presidential [archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span>

The importance of this authentic correspondence lies less in its contents than in its physical characteristics. Presidential letters preserve original signatures, formatting, and administrative conventions. ## Klass's copied-signature allegation

Science journalist and UFO sceptic Philip J. Klass made the signature comparison one of the central pillars of his critique of the MJ-12 papers.

According to Klass, the Truman signature appearing on the alleged September 1947 letter to Defence Secretary [James Forrestal]({{'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) was not merely similar to the signature on the authentic Truman-to-Bush letter—it was effectively identical. ## Why the argument focuses on source reuse

The significance of the copied-signature claim extends beyond handwriting comparison.

Forgery investigations commonly distinguish between fabricating an entirely fictional document and constructing one from authentic historical components. Reusing genuine stationery, authentic signatures, real bureaucratic terminology or genuine correspondence can make a fabricated document appear substantially more convincing because many individual elements withstand casual inspection.

Within the MJ-12 controversy, Bush occupies exactly this vulnerable position. Because he genuinely served at the intersection of science, defence and presidential policy, authentic White House correspondence involving Bush already existed in archival collections. If a forger wished to create documents suggesting secret presidential authority, genuine Truman–Bush material would provide plausible source material without requiring the invention of convincing presidential handwriting from scratch.

## Why the signature issue remains influential

The Truman-to-Bush correspondence occupies a distinctive place in debates over the alleged MJ-12 documents because it offers a specific, falsifiable claim rather than a general impression.

Unlike arguments based on whether a committee"could have existed," the signature allegation can be tested through comparison of known archival material with the disputed document. For that reason, it has remained one of the most frequently cited forensic objections in sceptical analyses of MJ-12.

It is also significant that the argument does not claim Bush himself participated in any fabrication. Rather, Bush's documented relationship with Truman is presented as the source from which an authentic presidential signature may have been extracted.
