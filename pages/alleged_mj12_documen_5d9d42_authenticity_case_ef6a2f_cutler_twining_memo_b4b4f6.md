## Introduction

The Cutler–Twining memorandum occupies a unique place in the MJ-12 controversy because, unlike the better-known briefing papers, it was discovered in the custody of the US National Archives rather than emerging solely through private circulation. To supporters of MJ-12, that archival [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}) has made it the strongest apparent piece of corroborating evidence for the alleged secret committee. Yet the same archival context has also generated the document's most serious [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) problems.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cutler Memo illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The central issue is not whether the memorandum physically exists—it does—but whether it behaves like an authentic 1954 [National Security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) Council (NSC) record. When archivists, [presidential library]({{'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) staff and government record specialists examined its filing, markings, paper, and relationship to other records, they found multiple inconsistencies. Individually, some might be explained away. Collectively, they have become one of the strongest reasons historians and document researchers remain sceptical that the memorandum is genuine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsJun 25, 2024 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries conc...</span></span></span>

## Why supporters treat the memo as crucial

The memorandum is dated 14 July 1954 and is presented as a note from Robert Cutler, Special Assistant to President Dwight D. Eisenhower, to General [Nathan Twining]({{'twining/' | relative_url }}). It refers to an upcoming briefing on an"NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project" scheduled for 16 July.

For advocates of MJ-12, this document matters because:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* it was located in an official government archive rather than arriving anonymously through UFO researchers;
* it mentions"NSC/MJ-12" by name;
* it appears to connect senior White House and military officials with the alleged project.</div>

Unlike the alleged Eisenhower briefing document, whose chain of custody begins with anonymous film sent to civilians, the Cutler memorandum can at least be traced to a specific archival file. That distinction explains why it is frequently presented as independent corroboration rather than merely another member of the same disputed document set.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsJun 25, 2024 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries conc...</span></span></span>

## The filing and register-number mismatch

The most immediate problem concerns where the memorandum was found and how it was filed.

According to the National Archives' reference report, the document was located in Record Group 341, Entry 267. That record series is organised by [Top Secret]({{'top-secret/' | relative_url }}) register numbers. Genuine documents in the series would normally bear such a registration number, allowing them to be tracked through the government's classified document control system.

The Cutler memorandum does not contain the required Top Secret register number.

This omission is significant because the filing system itself depends upon that numbering. If the document genuinely belonged in that series, its absence raises questions about how it entered the file. Conversely, if it was not part of the original registered series, its presence there requires an alternative explanation.

## The missing documentary trail

High-level government programmes rarely exist as isolated pieces of paper. Even highly classified activities usually generate administrative traces across multiple offices.

To test whether the memorandum reflected a genuine programme, National Archives researchers expanded their search well beyond the single folder. They examined records from:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the Office of the Secretary of Defense;
* the Joint Chiefs of Staff;
* Headquarters, US Air Force;
* related archival collections.</div>

The searches produced no corroborating documentation relating to MJ-12.

The Archives also sought information directly from the US Air Force, the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the National Security Council. Those enquiries likewise failed to uncover supporting records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsJun 25, 2024 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries conc...</span></span></span>

The absence of corroboration does not mathematically prove a forgery. [Classified records]({{'secret-trails/' | relative_url }}) can be destroyed, misfiled or remain undiscovered. Nevertheless, because the memorandum purports to refer to a White House-level special studies project involving senior officials, researchers generally expect some administrative footprint elsewhere in the federal record. The complete lack of such evidence substantially weakens the document's evidential value.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cutler Memo illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_authenticity_case_ef6a2f_cutler_twining_memo_b4b4f6-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The NSC meeting and classification problems

Two of the strongest objections involve the memorandum's apparent bureaucratic details rather than its substantive claims.

### The missing NSC meeting

The memorandum refers to a briefing scheduled for 16 July 1954.

The National Archives searched the official National Security Council meeting minute files and found no record of an NSC meeting on that date. Researchers also reviewed the July 1954 meeting minutes more broadly and found no mention of MJ-12 or Majestic. Searches of NSC indexes likewise failed to locate entries for MJ-12, Majestic, UFOs, flying saucers or similar terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsJun 25, 2024 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries conc...</span></span></span>

### The classification marking

Perhaps the most frequently cited technical objection concerns the phrase"Top Secret Restricted Information."

The National Security Council's Freedom of Information Office informed the National Archives that this marking did not come into use within the NSC until the Nixon administration, well after 1954. The Eisenhower Presidential Library independently confirmed that the marking was not used during the Eisenhower administration.

If those administrative histories are correct, the memorandum contains terminology that was not yet in official use on the date it supposedly originated. That represents a period-authenticity problem rather than merely an unusual formatting choice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsJun 25, 2024 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries conc...</span></span></span>

## Physical characteristics that raise additional questions

The National Archives also examined the memorandum as a physical object.

Its conservation specialists determined that the document is a ribbon copy prepared on diction onionskin paper. The Eisenhower Library compared it with authentic Cutler papers from the same period and observed notable differences.

Among their findings:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the memorandum lacks an official government letterhead;
* it has no government watermark;
* authentic Cutler documents produced while he served on the NSC staff were generally prepared on paper bearing an eagle watermark;
* many outgoing NSC documents were prepared on White House letterhead.</div>

These observations do not establish forgery by themselves, since legitimate copies could vary depending on how they were produced. ## What the memo can—and cannot—establish

The Cutler–Twining memorandum remains important because it is the only frequently cited MJ-12-related document discovered within government archival holdings rather than originating solely from private sources. That circumstance explains why it continues to receive disproportionate attention in discussions of the alleged MJ-12 papers.
