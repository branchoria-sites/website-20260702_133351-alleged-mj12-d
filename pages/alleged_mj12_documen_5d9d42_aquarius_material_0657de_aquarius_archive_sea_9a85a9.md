## Introduction

[Archive searches]({{'archive-searches/' | relative_url }}) occupy a central place in evaluating claims about Project Aquarius and Majestic-12 (MJ-12). The reason is straightforward: a genuine long-running classified programme would normally leave at least indirect traces in government record systems, filing indexes, routing logs, meeting schedules, correspondence registers or archival accession records, even if the substantive files remained classified. The archival evidence assembled over several decades does not provide such a trail. Instead, official searches by the US National Archives, presidential libraries, the [National Security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) Council record system and later reviews by other government bodies consistently failed to locate documentary support for Aquarius or a Majestic-12 organisation, apart from a single disputed memorandum that itself raised multiple archival concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Archive Trail illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This does not prove that no secret UFO programme ever existed. Rather, it establishes an important evidential point: the publicly documented archive record does not match what historians and archivists would ordinarily expect from the extensive bureaucratic system described in the alleged MJ-12 and [Aquarius]({{'aquarius/' | relative_url }}) papers.

## Which archives were searched?

The most comprehensive official account comes from the US National [Archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) and Records Administration (NARA), which received repeated public inquiries after MJ-12 documents began circulating during the 1980s.

Rather than looking only for a document labelled"MJ-12", archivists searched across multiple collections and record systems that should have contained references if such a programme had operated at senior government level. According to NARA, searches included:

* US Air Force records.
* Joint Chiefs of Staff records.
* National Security Council (NSC) policy paper indexes.
* NSC meeting minute indexes.
* Holdings at both the Harry S. Truman Library and the Dwight D. Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{'library-searches/' | relative_url }}).
* Subject indexes covering terms including"MJ-12","Majestic","UFO","flying saucers","extraterrestrial biological entities" and"Aquarius".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

This breadth matters because alleged MJ-12 material portrays the organisation as operating with presidential authority and involving senior military and intelligence officials. ## The one document that survived the searches

The official searches produced one apparent exception.

NARA reported finding a memorandum dated 14 July 1954 from Robert Cutler, Special Assistant to President Eisenhower, addressed to General [Nathan Twining]({{'twining/' | relative_url }}). The memo referred to an"NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project" briefing scheduled for 16 July. On its face, this appeared to be the only archival document mentioning"MJ-12".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

However, the memo immediately became the focus of closer archival scrutiny because its surrounding documentary context did not fit normal government record practice.

Among the issues identified by archivists were:

* the document lacked the standard control numbering normally associated with the record series in which it appeared;
* researchers could locate no supporting correspondence in related Department of Defense, Air Force or Joint Chiefs files;
* searches of National Security Council meeting records found no meeting matching the alleged briefing;
* no July 1954 NSC meeting minutes mentioned either MJ-12 or Majestic;
* the National Security Council advised that one classification marking found on the document was not used until the Nixon administration, making it anachronistic for a 1954 memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## What the negative results actually mean

Negative archival searches are sometimes misunderstood. They do not constitute absolute proof that something never existed.

Governments can destroy records, retain classified collections for decades or compartment programmes so tightly that surviving documentation is sparse. Historians therefore rarely argue from absence alone.

Instead, the evidential weight comes from the combination of independent negative findings across multiple archival systems.

In the Aquarius and MJ-12 case, researchers were not relying on a single missing file. They found no consistent trail across presidential libraries, National Security Council indexes, Air Force collections or Joint Chiefs records despite searching for multiple names and subject terms associated with the alleged programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why one memo cannot carry the entire system

Supporters of MJ-12 have sometimes argued that the Cutler memorandum is enough to establish the reality of a wider secret organisation.

From an archival perspective, that is a demanding claim.

If Aquarius and Majestic operated as described in the circulating documents, historians would ordinarily expect additional evidence such as:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* routing slips;
* filing register entries;
* meeting schedules;
* document distribution lists;
* cross-references in related departmental files;
* budgetary or administrative correspondence;
* successor or follow-up memoranda.</div>

Instead, the documentary pattern runs in the opposite direction: one isolated memo with unusual characteristics surrounded by an otherwise empty record trail. That is why archivists generally evaluate the memo as insufficient, by itself, to authenticate the broader alleged system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsAugust 15, 2016 — The National Archives has received numerous inquiries c...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Archive Trail illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_aquarius_material_0657de_aquarius_archive_sea_9a85a9-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Independent government reviews reached similar conclusions

The archival findings were later reinforced by other [official reviews]({{'official-reviews/' | relative_url }}).

The US Government Accountability Office (GAO), responding to congressional interest in the early 1990s, consulted agencies including the Air Force and the Information Security Oversight Office. Those agencies reported finding no records relating to Majestic-12 and concluded there was no evidence that the circulated MJ-12 papers originated as authentic executive branch documents. The GAO stated that nothing in its own work contradicted those conclusions. It also noted that archivists at the Truman and Eisenhower libraries had found no records matching the alleged Majestic-12 material or references to that designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/154832.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]These agencies added that they found&#x27;no &#x27;records in their files relating to Majestic 12.
