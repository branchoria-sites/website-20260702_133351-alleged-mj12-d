## Introduction

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) is one of the most important tools available to researchers examining UFO-related government records, including claims surrounding [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}). It can compel executive-branch agencies to search for and release existing records unless a lawful exemption applies. However, FOIA is often misunderstood as a mechanism that can prove or disprove extraordinary claims. It cannot do that. A successful FOIA request may uncover relevant documents, while an unsuccessful one may reflect missing records, lawful withholding, poor recordkeeping, or simply that no responsive records exist. Understanding these limits is essential when evaluating claims that rely heavily on what government [archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) do—or do not—contain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions...The FOIA does not require agencies to create new records or to conduct research...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="FOIA Limits illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## What FOIA Requests Can Ask For

FOIA applies to existing records held by federal executive agencies. A requester can ask for documents, emails, reports, photographs, memoranda or other recorded material, provided the request reasonably describes the records being sought. Agencies are required to conduct an appropriate search of their own holdings and determine whether responsive records can be released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/how-to.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on foia.gov.</span></span></span>

For UFO research, this means FOIA can be used to request:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Records relating to named investigations, projects or incidents.
* Correspondence mentioning specific people, dates or programmes.
* Classification reviews or declassification decisions.
* Technical reports, briefing papers or historical files already held by an agency.</div>

It cannot require an agency to:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Create a document that never existed.
* Answer questions such as"Did MJ-12 exist?"
* Conduct historical research on the requester's behalf.
* Draw conclusions from the records it possesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions...The FOIA does not require agencies to create new records or to conduct research...</span></span></span></div>

This distinction matters because many UFO debates concern alleged secret programmes rather than identifiable government records. FOIA is designed to obtain records, not official explanations.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/CTrH0xq5m7U" title="The CIA, UFOs and &quot;Material&quot;" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTrH0xq5m7U" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The CIA, UFOs and &quot;Material&quot;</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: Inside The Black Vault &middot; Views: 14.0K &middot; Uploaded: September 2022 &middot; Length: 20 minutes</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTrH0xq5m7U" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTrH0xq5m7U">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## Why Redactions and Agency Holdings Matter

Even when records are located, they may not be released in full. FOIA contains statutory exemptions allowing agencies to withhold information for reasons including [national security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}), intelligence sources and methods, personal privacy, and certain law-enforcement interests. In practice, released documents may contain substantial redactions while still confirming that records exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/foia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesFreedom of Information Act (FOIA)20 Dec 2022 — Federal agencies are required to disclose any information requested under...</span></span></span>

Equally important is that each agency searches only the records under its own control. There is no single government-wide UFO archive. A request sent to one department does not automatically search another department's files. Consequently, a"no records" response usually means that the receiving agency found no responsive records within the locations it was required to search—not that every part of the federal government lacks relevant material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions...The FOIA does not require agencies to create new records or to conduct research...</span></span></span>

Historical records can introduce another complication. Some records have been transferred to the National Archives, while others remain with the originating agency. Presidential records are also governed by different legal rules from ordinary agency records. Knowing where records should reside is therefore a key part of evaluating the significance of any FOIA response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/foia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesFreedom of Information Act (FOIA)20 Dec 2022 — Federal agencies are required to disclose any information requested under...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="FOIA Limits illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_archive_limits_3f5bb5_foia_limits_ufo_sear_37df79-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Common Mistakes in UFO FOIA Claims

FOIA responses are frequently cited in UFO discussions as definitive proof either for or against a claim. Several recurring mistakes can lead to conclusions that go beyond what the law actually establishes.

**Treating"no records" as proof that nothing happened.** A search may fail because records never existed, were never retained, were transferred elsewhere, were described differently, or because the request did not reasonably identify them. A negative response answers a narrower question than many commentators assume.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions...The FOIA does not require agencies to create new records or to conduct research...</span></span></span>

**Assuming redactions prove a conspiracy.** Classified or exempt material is routinely withheld under established statutory exemptions. Heavy redaction alone does not demonstrate that the underlying subject is extraordinary; it often reflects ordinary classification or privacy requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/foia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesFreedom of Information Act (FOIA)20 Dec 2022 — Federal agencies are required to disclose any information requested under...</span></span></span>

**Confusing agency silence with government-wide silence.** A response from one agency cannot automatically be extended to agencies that were not searched. Multi-agency historical claims require careful attention to which organisations were asked, what records were searched, and whether those agencies would reasonably be expected to possess the records in question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[FOIA.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions...The FOIA does not require agencies to create new records or to conduct research...</span></span></span>

**Expecting FOIA to authenticate disputed documents.** FOIA can locate related records, filing information or [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}), but [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) is ultimately evaluated through archival context, document examination, administrative consistency and corroborating evidence rather than by FOIA alone.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/OJGb7cvId5Y" title="DoD Refuses To Release Key Documents On UFO Office; ## Why FOIA Cannot Resolve the MJ-12 Debate by Itself

The alleged MJ-12 documents illustrate both the strengths and [limits]({{'limits/' | relative_url }}) of FOIA. If a genuine presidentially authorised inter-agency programme had generated ordinary administrative records, carefully targeted FOIA requests and archival searches would be expected to locate at least some supporting documentation. Conversely, the inability to locate such records does not logically prove that every claim about unidentified aerial phenomena is false.

Instead, FOIA contributes one piece of a broader evidential picture. Researchers compare agency responses with archival filing systems, classification practices, document provenance, contemporaneous correspondence and other historical sources. Where those independent lines of evidence consistently fail to support a document's claimed origin, confidence in its authenticity decreases. Where they align, confidence increases.

In other words, FOIA is a powerful transparency mechanism, but it is not an investigative tribunal.
