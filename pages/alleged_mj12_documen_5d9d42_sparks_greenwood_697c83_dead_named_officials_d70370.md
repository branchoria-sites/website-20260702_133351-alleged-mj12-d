## Introduction

One of the less obvious criticisms raised by researchers Brad Sparks and Barry Greenwood is that the alleged MJ-12 member list was unusually difficult to verify because every principal figure named in the documents had already died before the papers surfaced publicly in the mid-1980s. Their point was not that deceased officials automatically prove a forgery. Rather, they argued that a document naming only unavailable witnesses deprives investigators of one of the most direct methods of authentication: asking the people allegedly involved whether the committee existed or whether the documents resembled genuine records. In their view, this characteristic made the MJ-12 story unusually resistant to independent verification and increased the risk that impressive historical names could create an unwarranted appearance of [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Dead Names illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Who appeared on the alleged committee list?

The core MJ-12 documents named a group of well-known military leaders, scientists and government officials, including Roscoe Hillenkoetter, Vannevar Bush, [James Forrestal]({{'forrestal/' | relative_url }}), Nathan Twining, Hoyt Vandenberg, Donald Menzel, Gordon Gray and others who genuinely held influential [national security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) or scientific positions during the late 1940s and early 1950s. Their prominence helped make the alleged committee appear plausible at first glance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span>

By the time the so-called Eisenhower Briefing Document emerged anonymously in 1984 and was publicly announced in 1987, however, every person identified as an original committee member had died. Sparks and Greenwood explicitly highlighted this timing, describing it as"convenient" because none of the alleged participants could confirm, deny or clarify the claims attributed to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span>

This observation became part of their broader [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}) critique. Instead of relying on surviving participants, authenticated government files or corroborating administrative records, the story depended largely upon anonymous document leaks and retrospective interpretation.

## Why unavailable witnesses matter for provenance

Historical investigations rarely depend upon a single witness, but living participants can provide valuable evidence even when discussing classified programmes. They may:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* recognise authentic document formats or terminology;
* identify forged signatures or unusual drafting practices;
* explain how committees actually operated;
* deny participation in organisations that never existed; or
* direct researchers towards genuine supporting records.</div>

Because none of the alleged MJ-12 principals were alive when the documents surfaced, those avenues of verification disappeared immediately. Researchers therefore had to rely almost entirely on indirect evidence such as archival records, document analysis, provenance and corroborating paperwork.

This distinction is important because Sparks and Greenwood's criticism concerns methodology rather than speculation. Their argument is that the inability to question any alleged member increased the evidential burden on the documents themselves. Without living testimony, the documentary chain of custody and independent archival support became even more critical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Dead Names illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## How impressive names can create false confidence

The alleged member list included individuals whose careers genuinely intersected with military intelligence, scientific research and national security. That historical accuracy could easily give readers the impression that the documents themselves must also be authentic.

Sparks and Greenwood argued that this creates a potential trap. A skilled forger does not need to invent fictional officials. Instead, using real and distinguished historical figures can make fabricated documents appear credible precisely because readers recognise the names.

They also pointed out that Stanton Friedman later acknowledged that he, William Moore and Jaime Shandera had already identified nearly all of the eventual MJ-12 names through archival research before the anonymous film containing the documents arrived. From Sparks and Greenwood's perspective, this weakened the argument that the member list represented independent confirmation. Instead, they suggested it could reflect researchers' own prior expectations being echoed back to them through the leaked documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...&quot;The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12&quot; is a paper by US UFO r...</span></span></span>

Their critique therefore focuses less on whether the selected individuals were plausible candidates and more on whether the list supplied genuinely new information. If researchers had already assembled essentially the same roster through historical investigation, the appearance of those names in anonymous papers was not necessarily independent corroboration.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Dead Names illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_sparks_greenwood_697c83_dead_named_officials_d70370-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the lack of direct confirmation became more significant

The absence of surviving committee [members]({{'members/' | relative_url }}) did not stand alone. Sparks and Greenwood connected it with several other evidential problems:

* no verified government records establishing an MJ-12 committee;
* no authenticated administrative files showing its activities;
* no identified staff members or support structure;
* anonymous rather than documented provenance for the key papers; and
* no authenticated chain of custody linking the documents to official government [archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}).

The National Archives has likewise stated that extensive searches across Air Force, Joint Chiefs of Staff, National Security Council and presidential records found no evidence establishing an MJ-12 organisation beyond the disputed Cutler memorandum, which itself presents significant archival anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsThe National Archives has received numerous inquiries concerning document...</span></span></span>

## What this criticism does—and does not—claim

The fact that all named officials were deceased does not, by itself, prove the MJ-12 papers are forged. Genuine historical discoveries sometimes concern people who are no longer alive.

Instead, Sparks and Greenwood treated the issue as a problem of verification.
