## Introduction

One of the most significant tests of [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) was not whether they contained extraordinary claims, but whether those claims left an ordinary archival trail. Because the papers purported to originate at the highest levels of the Truman and Eisenhower administrations, archivists at the presidential libraries holding those administrations' records were well placed to look for corroborating evidence. Their searches did not simply seek copies of the disputed documents themselves. They also looked for the kinds of supporting records that genuine presidential [national-security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) decisions normally generate: appointment records, National Security Council (NSC) files, intelligence correspondence and references in related collections. The consistent outcome was negative. Both the presidential libraries and the National Archives reported that they could not locate records matching the alleged MJ-12 paper trail, a finding that became an important element in later assessments of the documents' authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/154832.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comments on Majestic 12 Material</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]July 28, 1995 — I&#x27;?:. ## Why Truman and Eisenhower records mattered

The alleged MJ-12 papers attribute the creation and oversight of a secret committee to President Harry S. Truman and later describe activities extending into the Eisenhower administration. If those claims were correct, researchers would reasonably expect traces to survive in the presidential records preserved at the two libraries.

These repositories are particularly relevant because they preserve not only presidential correspondence but also many of the documentary systems used to support executive decision-making. Truman's collections include sensitive national-security material, appointment records and the President's Secretary's Files, while the Eisenhower Library preserves extensive White House and NSC documentation from his administration. Genuine presidential actions involving classified policy frequently leave indirect evidence across multiple record series even when the underlying documents remain classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/truman-papers/harry-s-truman-paperspresidents-secretarys-files" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman LibraryHarry S. Truman Papers: President&#x27;s Secretary&#x27;s FilesThe President&#x27;s Secretary&#x27;s Files include national security and intell...</span></span></span>

For that reason, the libraries' role was not to determine whether UFO claims were true. Their task was to determine whether the documentary infrastructure expected for authentic presidential records existed.

## Which record groups were searched

According to both the National [Archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) and the Government Accountability Office (GAO), archivists examined a broad range of materials rather than looking only for documents labelled"MJ-12."

The searches included:

* Classified intelligence holdings available within the presidential collections.
* National Security Council records from the Truman and Eisenhower administrations.
* Presidential [appointment books]({{'limits/' | relative_url }}) and related scheduling records.
* Indexes to NSC policy papers and meeting minutes.
* References under multiple subject headings, including"MJ-12","Majestic","unidentified flying objects","UFO","flying saucers","extraterrestrial biological entities" and"[Aquarius]({{'aquarius/' | relative_url }})".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsSearches were made of the indexes to the NSC&#x27;s Policy Paper and Meeting M...</span></span></span>

The breadth of these searches matters because authentic executive-branch programmes generally generate more than one documentary trace. Even if a specific memorandum were missing, researchers would normally expect references in calendars, filing systems, meeting records or related correspondence.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_presidential_library_6abbfd-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Library Searches illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_presidential_library_6abbfd-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_presidential_library_6abbfd-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What the searches failed to find

The presidential library searches consistently failed to uncover evidence supporting the existence of the alleged MJ-12 programme.

## Why absent references were damaging

The importance of these negative findings lies in archival practice rather than in any single missing document.

Major presidential national-security decisions ordinarily produce a network of records created by different offices. Appointment books record meetings. NSC staff prepare agendas and minutes. Filing systems generate index entries. Correspondence is distributed among multiple officials. Even highly classified programmes typically leave administrative evidence of their existence.

The presidential library searches therefore tested whether the alleged MJ-12 papers fit within that broader documentary ecosystem. The answer was consistently no. Archivists did not merely report that they lacked one memorandum; they reported an absence of the expected supporting record trail across multiple collections and record systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsSearches were made of the indexes to the NSC&#x27;s Policy Paper and Meeting M...</span></span></span>

This distinction became especially important during the GAO review. Rather than asking whether UFO recovery programmes could theoretically have existed, GAO examined whether the alleged documents behaved like authentic executive-branch records. ## How these findings fit the broader executive-branch question

The presidential library searches did not prove that no secret programme ever existed, nor could archival silence definitively disprove every historical claim. Records can be lost, destroyed or remain classified.

However, within the specific question examined by GAO—whether the circulated MJ-12 documents appeared to be authentic executive-branch records—the libraries' findings carried considerable weight. Their searches were directed at precisely the collections where traces of presidential authorisation, NSC involvement and high-level coordination would most plausibly have survived.
