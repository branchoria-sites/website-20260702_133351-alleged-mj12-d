## Introduction

One of the central claims in the alleged MJ-12 papers is that President Harry S. Truman created the group by a"special classified executive order" dated 24 September 1947. That assertion matters because it attempts to give the alleged organisation the highest possible legal authority. However, when compared with how Truman's administration actually issued and recorded executive orders, the claim immediately raises questions. The supposed order is not identified in the way genuine Truman executive orders were, no matching executive order appears in the official sequence for that period, and no archival record has been found showing that such an order ever existed. While presidents have long issued classified directives outside the public executive order series, those directives still normally leave some administrative or archival footprint. The absence of such evidence is one of the reasons historians and archivists regard the alleged MJ-12 executive order with considerable scepticism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/executive-orders/harry-s-truman/1947" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register+2FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">harry s truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterFederal Register:: Executive OrdersIn 1947, Harry S. ## How Truman executive orders were normally identified

Executive orders under President Truman followed a well-established administrative system. Each received a sequential number assigned through the Federal Register process and became part of an uninterrupted historical series. During 1947, Truman signed executive orders numbered from 9818 through 9918, covering subjects ranging from military administration to labour disputes and government organisation. There is no gap corresponding to an unnumbered"special classified executive order" creating a secret scientific committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/executive-orders/harry-s-truman/1947" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">harry s truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterFederal Register:: Executive OrdersIn 1947, Harry S. Truman signed 103 Executive orders (from EO 9818 through EO 9918)...</span></span></span>

The MJ-12 documents instead refer to a document often labelled"Special Classified Executive Order 092447" or similar variations. That format is unlike genuine executive order numbering. Rather than using the established sequential system, the designation appears to be derived from the date—24 September 1947—rather than from the official executive order sequence.

## Why the alleged order number is suspicious

Several features of the alleged designation stand out when compared with authentic Truman documentation.

* **It does not match the official numbering system.** Truman's executive orders were identified by consecutive numbers such as Executive Order 9877 or Executive Order 9981, not by date-based identifiers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_executive_actions_by_Harry_S._Truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">List of executive actions by Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of executive actions by Harry S. Truman</span></span></span>
* **No corresponding executive order exists for 24 September 1947.** Researchers can account for the executive orders surrounding that date, and none establishes an organisation resembling MJ-12.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_executive_actions_by_Harry_S._Truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">List of executive actions by Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of executive actions by Harry S. Truman</span></span></span>
* **The wording itself is unusual.** The expression"Special Classified Executive Order" is not a recognised category within Truman's published executive order system. Genuine executive orders could concern classified matters, but they were still executive orders with official numbers rather than a separate class carrying a date-style identifier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/executive-orders/harry-s-truman/1947" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">harry s truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterFederal Register:: Executive OrdersIn 1947, Harry S. Truman signed 103 Executive orders (from EO 9818 through EO 9918)...</span></span></span>

This does not by itself prove that the document is forged. A defender might argue that an exceptionally sensitive presidential directive could have used a different internal format. The difficulty with that explanation is that no documentary evidence has been found showing Truman ever created such an alternative executive-order numbering system.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ogJplVuElLs" title="A secret history: Inside shadow intelligence, the MJ-12 documents | Reality Check" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ogJplVuElLs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">A secret history: Inside shadow intelligence, the MJ-12 documents | Reality Check</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: NewsNation &middot; Views: 247.8K &middot; Uploaded: April 2026 &middot; Length: 1 hour 20 minutes</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ogJplVuElLs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ogJplVuElLs">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## Could a classified presidential directive exist outside the executive order series?

Yes—but that is not the same thing as a classified executive order.

Presidents have often issued confidential instructions through memoranda, letters, military orders and, after the creation of the National Security Council in 1947, various forms of national security directives. These documents could remain classified for decades and were not necessarily published in the Federal Register.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_security_directive" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National security directive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National security directive</span></span></span>

That distinction is important because it provides a plausible mechanism for secret presidential instructions while simultaneously weakening the MJ-12 wording.

If Truman had wished to authorise an exceptionally sensitive intelligence project, historians generally expect he would have been more likely to use a classified presidential memorandum or directive than invent an entirely separate category of executive order. In other words, the existence of secret presidential instructions is historically normal; the specific form claimed by the MJ-12 papers is what appears anomalous.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_truman_authorisation_aafe47_executive_order_clai_a4d452-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Order Number illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_truman_authorisation_aafe47_executive_order_clai_a4d452-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_truman_authorisation_aafe47_executive_order_clai_a4d452-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What a classified directive would still be expected to leave behind

Even genuinely classified presidential directives usually generate administrative traces.

A presidential authorisation establishing a permanent inter-agency body would normally produce at least some combination of:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* White House filing records.
* Routing or registry entries.
* Security control numbers.
* Correspondence acknowledging receipt.
* References in departmental files.
* Later memoranda implementing or amending the directive.
* Archival accession records once records entered presidential or agency custody.</div>

## How this affects the credibility of the presidential-authority claim

The executive-order question does not rest on a single missing document. Instead, it combines several independent observations:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Truman's executive orders followed a documented sequential numbering system.
* The alleged MJ-12 order uses an unconventional date-style identifier.
* No official executive order matching that claim has been located.
* Secret presidential directives certainly existed, but they were generally distinct from executive orders and still tended to leave administrative evidence.
* Decades of archival searches have failed to uncover corroborating presidential records for the alleged order.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/executive-orders/harry-s-truman/1947" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Reagan Library+3Federal Register+3Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">harry s truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterFederal Register:: Executive OrdersIn 1947, Harry S. Truman signed 103 Executive orders (from EO 9818 through EO 9918)...</span></span></span></div>

Taken together, these factors do not prove with absolute certainty that no secret presidential instruction concerning unidentified aerial phenomena ever existed.
