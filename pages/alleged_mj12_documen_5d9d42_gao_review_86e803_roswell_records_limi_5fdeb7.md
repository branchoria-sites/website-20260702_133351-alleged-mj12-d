## Introduction

The loss of some records from Roswell Army Air Field is a genuine historical problem, but it does not resolve the separate question of whether [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) were authentic executive-branch records. Those are different issues. The General Accounting Office (GAO) confirmed in 1995 that certain Roswell administrative files and outgoing message records had been destroyed, creating unavoidable gaps in the documentary history of the base. At the same time, however, the GAO found no evidence that the alleged MJ-12 papers originated within the executive branch, and the agencies responsible for [classified records]({{'secret-trails/' | relative_url }}), archival preservation and security classifications reported no official record trail supporting them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — In our search for records concerning the Roswell crash, we learned that some government r...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Roswell Gap illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This distinction matters because MJ-12 claimed far more than a local military incident. The documents purported to describe a presidentially authorised, highly classified inter-agency programme involving senior officials, [national-security]({{'1947-context/' | relative_url }}) classification systems and long-term executive oversight. Missing records from one Army Air Field cannot, by themselves, account for the absence of evidence that such a programme ever existed across the wider federal [bureaucracy]({{'bureaucracy/' | relative_url }}).

## What Roswell records were missing

The GAO's investigation confirmed that some records relating to Roswell Army Air Field had been destroyed. Among the missing material were administrative records covering roughly 1945–1949 and outgoing message files for part of the late-1940s period. The available disposition documentation did not identify exactly who authorised their destruction or under what authority, leaving historians with a genuine archival gap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — In our search for records concerning the Roswell crash, we learned that some government r...</span></span></span>

Those missing files are significant because outgoing messages could have shown how local commanders reported events to higher headquarters. Their absence means that historians cannot reconstruct every communication sent from Roswell during the relevant period.

## How local gaps differ from executive records

The key analytical mistake is to treat missing base records as though they automatically explain the absence of records throughout the executive branch.

A local installation such as Roswell Army Air Field generated operational correspondence, administrative paperwork and routine communications. By contrast, the alleged MJ-12 documents describe:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* presidential approval;
* national-security classification;
* participation by multiple senior civilian and military officials;
* coordination across departments and agencies; and
* continuing management of an extraordinary classified programme.</div>

Claims at that level would ordinarily leave traces well beyond one air base. Even if some Roswell correspondence had been destroyed, records might reasonably be expected to survive in presidential files, departmental registries, classification systems, headquarters correspondence, archival holdings or related agency collections.

This broader expectation explains why the GAO sought information from organisations beyond Roswell itself. Rather than asking only what remained at the base, it asked whether agencies responsible for classified information and federal records had any evidence that the MJ-12 documents were authentic executive-branch creations. Their consistent answer was that they did not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views on the authenticity of Majestic 12, allegedly a classifie...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Roswell Gap illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why MJ-12 needed more than base files

The alleged MJ-12 papers depend on a chain of official authority extending far beyond New Mexico.

For the documents to be genuine, several propositions would all need support:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the executive branch would have had to create the committee or programme described;
* classified document-control systems would have had to handle the records;
* multiple agencies would have participated in or received related communications; and
* some corroborating administrative trail would be expected to survive outside Roswell.</div>

Instead, the GAO reported that the Information Security Oversight Office, the Air Force and the National [Archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}) had no evidence that the circulated MJ-12 material represented authentic executive-branch documents. Their knowledge of MJ-12 came from material submitted by private individuals rather than from government records already held within official files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views on the authenticity of Majestic 12, allegedly a classifie...</span></span></span>

That finding addresses a fundamentally different question from whether Roswell's own records are incomplete. Destroyed local files cannot explain why agencies responsible for classification policy, records management and federal archives also lacked evidence of the alleged programme.

## What the Roswell gap can and cannot show

The missing Roswell records do support one modest conclusion: historians cannot prove that every potentially relevant document survived. Any reconstruction of the 1947 events must acknowledge that some original source material has been lost. The GAO itself recognised this limitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — In our search for records concerning the Roswell crash, we learned that some government r...</span></span></span>

However, the archival gap does **not** establish several stronger claims that are sometimes inferred:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It does not demonstrate that MJ-12 existed.
* It does not show that the destroyed files contained MJ-12 documents.
* It does not explain the absence of corroboration from other executive agencies.
* It does not convert disputed photocopied documents into authenticated government records.</div>

This distinction reflects a basic principle of historical reasoning. Missing evidence can prevent definitive conclusions about some events, but it does not automatically validate a separate claim requiring its own independent documentation.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Roswell Gap illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_gao_review_86e803_roswell_records_limi_5fdeb7-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the distinction remains important

The Roswell record losses remain relevant because they limit what can be known about communications originating from the base in 1947. They are therefore an authentic part of the historical record and should not be dismissed.
