## Introduction

The 1988 Air Force Office of Special Investigations ([AFOSI]({{'afosi/' | relative_url }})) assessment that the alleged MJ-12 papers were"completely bogus" was not the last official review of the documents. Several years later, the U.S. General Accounting Office (GAO, now the Government Accountability Office) broadened the question by asking whether the alleged MJ-12 material left any trace in the executive branch's records system. Rather than attempting to prove who created the documents, the GAO examined whether they had the characteristics of genuine government records. Its conclusion reinforced the earlier AFOSI position: agencies could find no evidence that the material originated within the executive branch, and an apparently related 1980 Air Force message containing the phrase"MJ Twelve" was itself judged to be a forgery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="GAO Check illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This later review mattered because it shifted the discussion from document appearance to documentary [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}). Instead of asking whether the papers looked authentic, investigators asked whether authentic government records existed behind them.

## What GAO asked agencies to look for

The GAO's 1995 comments on Majestic 12 arose during its wider review of government records relating to the Roswell investigation. As part of that work, the GAO sought information from agencies that would reasonably be expected to possess or manage genuine executive-branch records if the alleged MJ-12 organisation or its documents had ever existed. These included the Information Security Oversight Office (ISOO), the U.S. Air Force and the National [Archives]({{'archives/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span>

[The agencies consistently reported that:](#endnote-1"Snippet: GAO<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/154832.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span> 28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies' views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua")<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* their knowledge of MJ-12 came only from copies supplied by private individuals rather than from government files;
* they found no records relating to Majestic 12 within their own holdings;
* searches of relevant archival collections did not locate original executive-branch records matching the alleged documents; and
* there was no evidence that the circulated MJ-12 papers were authentic government documents created within the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span></div>

Importantly, the GAO did not claim to have proved a particular individual forged the papers. Instead, it reported that the expected documentary trail of authentic federal records was absent.

## Why missing executive-branch provenance mattered

For historians and archivists, provenance—the documented origin and custody of a record—is a fundamental test of [authenticity]({{'authenticity/' | relative_url }}). Genuine classified documents normally leave traces through creation, filing, archival transfer or records-management systems, even when their contents remain classified.

The GAO found no such institutional trail for the MJ-12 material. According to the agencies it consulted, the documents entered government awareness only after private researchers circulated copies. They were not discovered through official archives, agency files or [classified records]({{'secret-trails/' | relative_url }}) repositories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span>

That finding strengthened the earlier AFOSI assessment in an important way. AFOSI had evaluated the documents operationally and concluded they were bogus. The GAO, by contrast, approached the issue from a records-management perspective and likewise found no evidence that the papers originated as authentic executive-branch records. The two reviews therefore reached compatible conclusions through different investigative methods.

The National Archives independently described similar search efforts, noting extensive examinations of Air Force, Joint Chiefs of Staff and [presidential library]({{'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) records in response to repeated public enquiries about MJ-12, without locating authentic government versions of the alleged documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsThe National Archives has received numerous inquiries concerning document...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="GAO Check illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_afosi_rejection_0a0eb4_gao_reinforced_bogus_035924-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## How the 1980 Air Force message added a second forgery trail

One of the more significant observations in the GAO's comments concerns a separate piece of purported evidence often cited by MJ-12 proponents: a 1980 Air Force message containing the words"MJ Twelve."

Rather than treating that message as corroboration, the GAO reported that the Air Force had determined the [1980 message]({{'1980-message/' | relative_url }}) itself to be a forgery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span>

This point is notable because it addresses a different category of evidence from the better-known Eisenhower briefing document. Even if someone argued that one alleged MJ-12 document remained open to debate, the Air Force concluded that another document used to support the same narrative was independently fabricated.

The result was a second, separate official rejection within the same body of alleged evidence:

* the principal MJ-12 papers lacked any demonstrable executive-branch provenance; and
* an additional Air Force communication invoking"MJ Twelve" was itself judged to be forged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAO[Comments on Majestic 12 Material]28 Jul 1995 — GAO examined agencies&#x27; views there was no evidence that the material constituted actua...</span></span></span>

These findings did not identify the forger or explain the motives behind the documents' creation, but they reduced the evidential value of using multiple purported government papers to corroborate one another.

## What the GAO review did—and did not—establish

The GAO's work is sometimes misunderstood as a comprehensive investigation into every aspect of MJ-12. It was considerably narrower.

The review did **not** conclude that every UFO claim connected with Roswell had been investigated or disproved. Nor did it claim to know who fabricated the MJ-12 papers.

Instead, its contribution was more specific:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* it tested whether the alleged documents had a verifiable executive-branch records history;
* it compared responses from multiple federal record-holding agencies;
* it found no official documentary provenance supporting the alleged MJ-12 material; and
