## Introduction

The Albuquerque postmark on the anonymous package sent to Jaime Shandera became one of the most discussed physical details in [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) story. By itself, a postmark proves only where a package entered the postal system. Yet because Albuquerque sits in the same state as Roswell and near major military installations associated with Cold War aerospace research, the New Mexico origin immediately suggested a connection to the mythology surrounding the 1947 Roswell incident. For supporters, this geographical detail hinted that the leak might have originated from someone with access to classified material in New Mexico. For sceptics, the same detail appeared almost too appropriate, making the package look carefully designed to evoke existing UFO narratives rather than authenticate the documents themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Postmark illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Why New Mexico mattered to the story

When Shandera reported receiving the undeveloped 35 mm film in December 1984, accounts consistently described the package as carrying an Albuquerque, New Mexico postmark while lacking any return address or sender identification. That combination made the mailing appear mysterious but offered almost no forensic value beyond its point of posting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic 12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, New Mexico carried exceptional symbolic weight within [UFO culture]({{'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}):<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Roswell was the location of the famous 1947 incident around which the alleged MJ-12 documents were constructed.
* Albuquerque is home to nearby military and scientific facilities, including Kirtland Air Force Base and Sandia laboratories, long associated in public imagination with advanced military technology.
* During the late 1970s and early 1980s, New Mexico had already become central to several UFO narratives involving military secrecy and alleged intelligence sources.</div><div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/kJQ_FsmU19g" title="The Airforce UFO Cover Up That Drove a Man INSANE | They&#x27;re LYING to US" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kJQ_FsmU19g" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Airforce UFO Cover Up That Drove a Man INSANE | They&#x27;re LYING to US</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: The Why Files &middot; Views: 5.4M &middot; Uploaded: September 2022 &middot; Length: 33 minutes</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kJQ_FsmU19g" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kJQ_FsmU19g">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## How the postmark strengthened the leak narrative

The postmark functioned less as evidence than as narrative framing.

Unlike an authenticated government archive, the anonymous mailing provided no chain of custody. There was no identifiable sender, no accompanying explanation and no original paper documents available for examination. The Albuquerque cancellation therefore became one of the few tangible facts surrounding the package.

Within UFO [research circles]({{'research-circle/' | relative_url }}), that single fact was often interpreted in several ways:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It implied that the sender may have been located near military or intelligence facilities in New Mexico.
* It appeared consistent with claims that insiders were leaking sensitive material while concealing their identities.
* It complemented the Roswell subject matter without explicitly identifying any military installation or government office.</div>

Some later accounts from researchers involved in the affair suggested they viewed the Albuquerque origin as another indication that anonymous"inside sources" might have been involved. The geographical connection therefore helped the package resemble a genuine intelligence leak despite offering no direct evidence about who actually mailed it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/24214339/The_Secret_Pratt_Tapes_and_the_Origins_of_MJ_12_Brad_Sparks_and_Barry_Greenwood_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Pratt Tapes and the Origins of MJ-12 (Brad...On December 11, 1984, Jaime Shandera received a 35 mm roll of film conta...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 11, 1984</span></span></span>

Importantly, postal evidence cannot establish authorship. A package can be mailed from any city regardless of where its contents were created. As a result, the postmark has always been circumstantial rather than authenticating evidence.<img src="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Postmark illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_shandera_film_roll_f82fd7_albuquerque_postmark_c3109d-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why sceptics saw a planted signal

Critics reached almost the opposite conclusion from the same piece of evidence.

Rather than viewing Albuquerque as an authentic clue, sceptics argued that it functioned as an obvious narrative device. If someone wanted anonymous documents about Roswell to appear convincing, choosing a New Mexico postmark would immediately create the desired association without proving anything substantive.

Investigators such as Philip J. Klass repeatedly argued that many aspects of the MJ-12 affair seemed designed to maximise dramatic effect while minimising opportunities for verification. From this perspective, the Albuquerque mailing fit a broader pattern:

* it created an emotional link to Roswell;
* it suggested insider access without identifying any source;
* it encouraged speculation while providing no verifiable [provenance]({{'provenance/' | relative_url }}).<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/YfMw75ZWVTI" title="The Secret History of Majestic 12" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YfMw75ZWVTI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Secret History of Majestic 12</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: Skeptoid Media</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YfMw75ZWVTI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YfMw75ZWVTI">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## What the postmark can and cannot demonstrate

From an evidential standpoint, the Albuquerque postmark occupies an unusual position.

It is a genuine physical characteristic of the reported mailing, but it supports only a very limited conclusion: that the package was processed through Albuquerque's postal system. It does **not** establish:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* who mailed the package;
* where the photographed documents originated;
* whether any government employee was involved;
* whether the documents themselves were authentic.</div>

Its importance instead lies in how effectively it connected the anonymous film to an already powerful cultural narrative centred on Roswell and government secrecy.
